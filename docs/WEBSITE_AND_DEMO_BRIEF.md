## Proposed page structure and copy

### Opening

Life Pilot
For when life throws a whole lot of life at you.

Life Pilot is a personal life-navigation platform designed to help people understand how the connected parts of life affect one another.

See what matters. Plan what's next. Take the next practical step.

Status line: Life Pilot is in development. Its first test is focused on explainable signals, controlled scenarios and practical next steps.

### The problem

A change in work, income, a bill or a household commitment can affect much more than one part of life. Information is often scattered across services, documents, calendars and conversations. Life Pilot is being developed to bring those connections into a clearer view.

### How the product is organised

Home Life covers everyday household obligations and administration. Work Life connects employment and earning activity. Family Life supports caring responsibilities and trusted relationships. Future Life helps compare controlled scenarios and future decisions.

Money is a signal across all four areas. Shared Life Tools bring activity, calendar, documents and people into the relevant context.

### The Life Signal

The Life Signal is designed to show what matters now, what needs attention and what practical action could follow. A signal should explain the information and assumptions behind it. Missing information must be visible.

### Controlled planning

The first test uses a fixed set of scenarios with limited inputs. It explores how a defined change affects the picture. It does not predict the future or replace professional advice.

### Trust and user control

Life Pilot is being designed around user agency, respectful language and consent. Organisation-supported access must not become surveillance. Individual information must not be shared by default.

### Development and partnership

The commercial direction is structured testing with government, NGOs, NFPs and community organisations. The first demonstration will use fictional information. Real-user testing will require a separately approved safety and consent process.

No partner logos, testimonials, impact statistics or customer counts may appear without verification and approval.

## Demonstration script

Before the session: verify the approved revision, fictional dataset, reset behaviour, display size and facilitator. Close unrelated tabs and notifications. Confirm no real accounts or integrations are connected.

1. Explain that this is a development demonstration using fictional information, not personal advice.
2. Open the household view and identify the four Core Life Areas.
3. Select one attention signal. Ask what the viewer believes it means before showing the explanation.
4. Show the contributing facts, rule and assumptions. Ask what information would help the viewer trust or challenge it.
5. Open the bill-timing scenario. Change one permitted input, compare outcomes and explain the difference.
6. Reset the scenario and confirm the original values return.
7. Show the connection between a signal, practical task and relevant Life Tool.
8. Explain privacy and what is not shared. Do not demonstrate fictional consent as if it were a production control.
9. Show missing-input behaviour.
10. Close with what remains untested and the next planned validation step.

## Feedback prompts

What did you think needed attention? Which facts or assumptions were unclear? Could you explain why the scenario changed? Was the proposed next step practical? Did any wording feel judgmental? What did you believe another organisation could see?

Record observations separately from interpretations. Do not turn a small convenience sample into a claim about population outcomes. No participant identity or sensitive personal story is needed for a product-language discussion.

## Demo stop conditions

## Go to market working strategy

Start with a bounded validation partnership, not a broad launch. Prioritise an organisation with a clear service context, staff able to support safe testing, respect for user-controlled sharing and willingness to challenge assumptions. Employment, housing and financial-wellbeing services are candidate segments, not confirmed buyers.

Discovery should test three hypotheses: the end user understands connected pressures; a supported organisation can use the tool without surveillance or case-management drift; and a funding buyer sees sufficient value to support access.

Proposed sequence: synthetic demonstration and language review; approved partner discovery; separately approved small pilot; review usefulness, trust and operating costs; only then test pricing and procurement. No recruitment or outreach occurs under this brief.

Measures should include task completion, explanation comprehension, scenario consistency, trust understanding, setup effort, repeat use in a later pilot, staff support time and willingness to fund a defined use case. Set targets with the agreed pilot protocol, not after seeing results.

Commercial options remain partner-funded cohort access, social-impact licensing and procurement-supported programmes. Direct-user access is possible but not the primary framing. Price, market size, customer acquisition cost, retention and revenue forecasts require evidence and remain unclaimed.
