## Foundation additions for approval

### Mission

Proposed mission: Help people understand the connected pressures and possibilities in everyday life, so they can make informed choices and take the next practical step without shame or overwhelm.

### Vision

Proposed vision: A future in which people and households can see how the parts of life connect, preserve their options when pressure rises, and access practical support while retaining control of their information and decisions.

These are additions for Gary's approval. They do not change the locked product purpose or approved messaging:
- For when life throws a whole lot of life at you.
- See what matters. Plan what's next. Take the next practical step.
- Life Pilot - Real Life Signal.

### Product and commercial purpose

Life Pilot is a personal life-navigation platform. Life Pilot Starter is its always-on commercial core. Home Life, Work Life, Family Life and Future Life are its four Core Life Areas. Money and support work across those areas. Activity, calendar, documents/evidence, people/providers and other approved Life Tools support the shared experience.

Government, NGOs, NFPs, community organisations and relevant support services are the primary commercial customer context. The person or household is the end user. Payment by an organisation does not confer access to an individual's information.

The product helps users connect facts, see explainable signals, compare controlled scenarios and identify practical next steps. It does not replace qualified financial, health, legal or social support. No financial, employment, health or crisis outcome is represented as proven.

## Proposed first test version

This is a bounded first test within the commercial MVP direction, not a claim to have delivered the complete commercial MVP.

The first executable test should use synthetic adult personas and fictional events only. It should demonstrate the Life Signal, the four areas, a minimum coherent set of Life Tools and controlled scenario comparison. No real household or support-client data, live integrations, payments, external messages, partner access or production authentication changes are included.

An internal synthetic test precedes any real-user pilot. Real-user recruitment, consent materials, hosting, storage, access, retention and support procedures require a later decision.

### Required journeys and acceptance evidence

FT01 Get clear: Open a fictional household and identify Home Life, Work Life, Family Life and Future Life. Money must appear through relevant signals, never as a fifth area. Evidence: labelled screenshots and a terminology check across all primary screens.

FT02 Understand attention: Select a signal and see the contributing facts, date range, rule, missing inputs and a practical next step. Evidence: each displayed signal traced to a scenario fixture and approved rule. Missing information must be labelled unknown, not silently treated as zero.

FT03 Plan ahead: Select a scenario from a fixed library, adjust only its permitted inputs, compare baseline and changed results and reset. Evidence: expected-versus-actual values, repeated-run consistency and reset verification.

FT04 Step forward: Choose a suggested practical action and show its relationship to the signal. Record it within the demo only. Evidence: completed action state and return-to-dashboard behaviour. The system must not contact a third party or act on the user's behalf.

FT05 Use Life Tools: Show activity, calendar, documents/evidence and people/providers within the chosen journey. Documents and people are fictional examples. Evidence: navigation and record association checks; no external calendar or inbox connection.

FT06 Preserve agency: Show what is private and make clear that no partner receives information. Any sharing concept must be a labelled non-functional demonstration unless separately approved. Evidence: user-facing explanation and network/data-flow review.

FT07 Handle failure: Show missing values, invalid inputs, empty records and unavailable actions without an unexplained score or false certainty. Evidence: boundary tests and screenshots.

FT08 Access the experience: Complete the essential flow with keyboard controls and visible focus; check readable labels, contrast and mobile-width layout. Evidence: manual accessibility notes and captured viewport sizes. Full certification is not implied.

### Proposed scenario fixtures

SC01 Bill timing: Fictional available funds 600 units, planned income 400 units on day 8 and a bill of 350 units due on day 5. A cash-timing comparison must show 250 units after the bill and 650 after the later income, with any other commitments explicitly excluded. Moving income after day 8 must not alter the day-5 amount. This is a test fixture, not financial advice.

SC02 Income interruption: Fictional weekly income changes from 700 to 500 units over two weeks. Holding all other inputs fixed produces a 400-unit difference across that horizon. The product must explain the horizon and avoid extrapolating indefinitely.

SC03 Competing commitments: A work commitment and caring appointment overlap. Display the overlap, the affected Work Life and Family Life contexts, and an option to prepare a rescheduling task. Do not assume whose commitment should take priority or send a message.

Final fixture fields, rule precedence, rounding, currency display and date/time handling must be approved in the build packet. Reuse verified existing scenario logic where compatible; do not replace it solely to match these examples.

### Exclusions

No open-ended AI scenarios, autonomous choices, professional advice, eligibility decisions, partner dashboard, surveillance, real-user onboarding, broad assistant suite, activated-layer workflows or parked-product implementation. No MYOH renaming, private-data copying, schema/migration/auth/permission change, new deployment or public website is authorised by document preparation.

### Exit criteria

All approved essential journeys pass on the submitted revision. Every numerical scenario matches independently calculated expectations, with repeatability and missing-input behaviour recorded. No unresolved blocker or material defect may pass silently. Minor defects require a named owner and explicit disposition. Privacy leakage, unexplained recommendations and accidental external actions are blockers.

Evidence includes revision, environment, dataset version, test steps, expected and actual result, tester, date, screenshots where useful and defect links. Tests not run are labelled not run. A static prototype cannot be labelled a tested application.
